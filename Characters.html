鐵甲戰士

Ironclad

StrengthExhaustBlockSelf-Damage

The Ironclad is one of five playable characters in Slay the Spire 2 and one of three returning from the original game. His playstyle revolves around Strength scaling, self-damage, Vulnerable synergies, and Exhausting cards. He is the most straightforward character and a good one to learn the game with.

Tier Lists for Ironclad

What's Changed in Slay the Spire 2?

There are a few big changes:

  • Vulnerable is now a full archetype complete with cards that revolve around applying, stacking, and rewarding the effect.
  • Self-damage is a viable package. A bunch of cards lean into losing HP as a cost or trigger.
  • Strength scaling is a bit more refined this time around. Still intact, but more measured.

Understanding the Ironclad

The Ironclad's starting Relic, Burning Blood(燃燒之血), heals him for 6 HP after every combat. This is a very strong Relic, as access to healing lets you mitigate losses from bad combat scenarios and pull further ahead in fights that go well without having to protect your health total.

To compensate for this powerful Relic, he starts with a simple deck of 5 Strikes(打擊), 4 Defends(防禦), and only one unique card, Bash(猛擊). It fits his character identity well, applying Vulnerable and leaning into his focus on attack damage.

The Ironclad starts with a deck weighted towards damage and doesn't have many good Block options. This leads him to rely on Burning Blood(燃燒之血) and defeating enemies quickly.

Strong Commons to Pick With the Ironclad

Attacks

A 0-cost attack like Anger(怒火) is very valuable in the early game. It lets you increase your output and make use of the five cards you draw per turn. Breakthrough(突破) is a decent option for multi-enemy fights, even without synergies. Headbutt(頭槌) does okay damage for its Energy cost and also has draw-sequencing utility for the late game.

Iron Wave(鐵斬波) doesn’t offer a lot of either Block or damage, and it will bloat your deck later, but it will save you health in Act 1 while you look for premium cards.

Pommel Strike(劍柄打擊) is a great Common, especially if you can upgrade it, increasing your card draw and consistency. Molten Fist(熔融之拳) offers good damage for 1 Energy with the upside of leading into potential Vulnerable synergies. Perfected Strike(完美打擊) does good frontloaded damage and helps to defeat early Elites. You don't have to commit to a "Strike(打擊) deck" because you picked one early.

Skills

Bloodletting(放血) is a great Energy source if you have card draw or expensive cards. Armaments(武裝) can be okay in its base form if you need some more Block. It is also good when upgraded in longer fights. Shrug It Off(聳肩帶過) does a lot for its cost and is straightforwardly good, while Tremble(戰慄) is a much more efficient way to apply Vulnerable than Bash(猛擊). Blood Wall(血牆)’s 14 Block for 2 Energy is good enough on its own and it can also synergize with other self-damage cards.

Strong Uncommons to Pick With the Ironclad

Attacks

Like Anger(怒火), Bully(恃強凌弱) also benefits from 0-cost Attacks being good early. This also has the upside of becoming a direction to build around later and not falling off. Spite(怨恨) is also worth taking early before you have a build direction, even without synergies from the first few fights.

Whirlwind(旋風斬) is a great Attack for multi-enemy fights and it has excellent synergy with the Ironclad’s different ways of generating Energy. Dismantle(拆卸) is conditional damage but it deals 24 damage for 1 Energy in base form when its condition is met: Definitely good early with any Vulnerable applicator.

Hemokinesis(御血術) can solve most of your damage issues by itself in the early game. Uppercut(上勾拳) is the Ironclad’s only way to apply Weak, making it a standout card. Stomp(踐踏) is a good source of damage and you can reliably count it as a 0-cost card.

Skills

Battle Trance(戰鬥專注) is straightforward: free card draw is good in every card game. Pick it, but be mindful that every extra copy becomes progressively less impressive. Being able to Block while dealing damage is great, so Rage(狂怒) is good if you have ways to play multiple Attacks in a turn consistently. Burning Pact(燃燒契約) is card draw that thins your deck, improving it for future cycles.

Colossus(巨像) was previously a Rare card. Weak is already so good, so this boosted version of the effect is excellent. An upgraded Dominate(主宰) is good even without additional Vulnerable synergies, as any Strength source, especially a scalable one, is very useful for the Ironclad. Infernal Blade(地獄之刃) can be very good when upgraded. The early game is all about increasing your damage output, so having a 0-cost card that can turn into any Ironclad Attack for free is great.

Taunt(嘲諷) offers useful Block in the early game and an additional way to apply Vulnerable. Flame Barrier(火焰屏障) is a premium Block card that also does damage. Evil Eye(邪眼) also offers good Block by itself and an outstanding amount if you can activate its effect.

Powers

Inferno(煉獄) is a great card that turns the downside of many strong class cards into a clear upside. Rupture(綻裂) can be good if you have multiple self-damage cards, but unlike Inferno(煉獄), it isn't strong enough to include on its own. Stampede(奔踏) can be good if you need more damage output and if you can get an upgrade on it to reduce its cost.

The Ironclad’s exclusive relics reinforce the same build pillars as the card pool. There’s very clear support for Strength scaling, Exhaust synergies, Vulnerable, and self-damage with very little overlap between them.

Detailed Build Directions for the Ironclad

Exploiting synergies around Vulnerable, Exhaust, self-damage, and Block forms the Ironclad’s best-defined card packages. Keep in mind that these directions aren't mutually exclusive. Often, the correct decision is to mix strengths and packages from different directions to have the best chance of success. Strength buffs help make the most out of Vulnerable and self-damage strategies.

Strike(打擊) Slop

Strikes(打擊) have gotten a cool new tool in Slay the Spire 2 with Hellraiser(地獄狂徒), which plays each Strike(打擊) you draw for free. This can lead to a somewhat degenerate play pattern where, if you draw enough cards, the combat is pretty much played for you. Hence the name: Strike(打擊) Slop. The key card when playing around this is Pommel Strike(劍柄打擊), when you have enough upgraded Pommel Strike(劍柄打擊)s you can go through most of your deck, or even loop a near-infinite number of them.

The other big payoff, as always, is Perfected Strike(完美打擊). Even if you don't have the perfect setup with multiple upgraded Pommel Strikes(劍柄打擊), this can still be a strong package if you have other means to amplify their damage.

Vulnerable Builds

Vulnerable has become a full-fledged direction in Slay the Spire 2, with many kinds of synergistic cards that let you benefit from its application.

Dominate(主宰) is the best payoff alongside Bully(恃強凌弱). Tremble(戰慄) is an efficient source of Vulnerable, applying 3 for 1 Energy. Molten Fist(熔融之拳) is okay if needed, but it Exhausts, so it’s not the best early. Cruelty(殘酷) doubles the effect, which is great, especially if you can combine it with Paper Phrog(紙異蛙). Vicious(兇狠) is also a good payoff card for the package.

Exhaust Builds

Exhaust builds synergize with Corruption(腐化), making all Skills cost 0 Energy but "burning" them after use. While the synergies are very powerful, getting started with building them out is tough. Pick the generally good cards that also happen to exhaust like Fiend Fire(惡魔之焰), Second Wind(重振精神), Burning Pact(燃燒契約), etc. and then pick the Powers that benefit from it, like Feel No Pain(無懼疼痛) and Dark Embrace(黑暗之擁) and see whether it’s worth leaning into further.

Ashen Strike(灰燼打擊) could also be a reason to lean more into Exhaust from that point on. Burning Pact(燃燒契約) can lead to some really streamlined strategies and is great support for most build directions. Evil Eye(邪眼), as previously discussed, is strong as-is and excellent when you can take advantage of its Exhaust synergy. True Grit(堅毅不屈) is usually also considered to be a strong Exhaust card when upgraded.

Second Wind(重振精神) can help speed up Exhausting through your deck while providing a lot of Block. When supported by targeted Exhaust for non-Skills, this can lead to very powerful deck cycles. Pact’s End is excellent damage and a great way to quickly work through your deck.

Block Builds

Barricade(壁壘) is the key card for Block builds that like to stack Block and then use cards like Body Slam(全身砸擊) to deal massive damage.

Sometimes, you will see a few premium Block cards early, along with a Body Slam(全身砸擊). The new Rare card, Unmovable(堅定不移), is more than enough to support this Attack by itself, and it gets even better if you can use cards like Burning Pact(燃燒契約) to exhaust your Attacks and smaller Blocks while quickly drawing towards your upgraded 0-cost Body Slam(全身砸擊).

Prolong(延長) is a great Colorless choice for Block builds, and, of course, all related Relics like Pael's Legion or Vambrace(臂鎧) can be a strong signal to consider this build direction.

Self-Damage Builds

With the right cards, you can make a build around damaging yourself, too. Rupture(綻裂) and Inferno(煉獄) are critical cards, with other noteworthy cards being Bloodletting(放血), Crimson Mantle(緋紅披風), Offering(祭品), and Tear Asunder(撕碎). As secondary choices for this package, consider Blood Wall(血牆), and Hemokinesis(御血術).

靜默獵手

Silent

PoisonShivSly

The Silent is one of three returning characters in Slay the Spire 2. She’s built around three core archetypes: stacking Poison to kill enemies over time, flooding the board with free Shiv attacks, or leaning into discard payoffs with Sly. 

Tier Lists for Silent

What's Changed in Slay the Spire 2

The most meaningful addition in Slay the Spire 2 is the Sly keyword, which turns discarding cards into a key payoff. This makes cards like Reflex(反射神經) and Tactician(戰術家) even stronger than they were in the original game. The Weak keyword also grew in relevance thanks to cards like Tracking(追蹤).

Understanding the Silent

The Silent is a very strong character with some of the best defensive and consistency options in the game. Her damage package is where she can falter. The tools covered in this guide, whether those are Shivs for immediate damage, Poison for consistent pressure, or Sly for efficiency and output, are not mutually exclusive.

The best Silent runs will often combine elements of all three alongside other, smaller synergy packages, using whichever cards your rewards offer to solve the problems your deck still has.

Strong Commons to Pick With the Silent

Frontload

Leading Strike(先導打擊), Poisoned Stab(帶毒刺擊), and Blade Dance(刀刃之舞) all deal over 10 damage for 1 Energy, making them strong early pickups regardless of your eventual direction. Slice(切割) is a useful 0-cost Attack. Dagger Throw(投擲匕首) is also a nice card early, as it deals a respectable amount of damage while also serving as a Sly enabler. Having some Poison to increase your total damage output is often necessary as enemy health pools grow.

Defensive

Deflect(偏轉) blocks for 0 Energy, making it an efficient way to get value out of your increased hand size. Backflip(後空翻) smooths out your draws by replacing itself with 2 cards. Dodge and Roll(閃躲翻滾) provides consistent Block that improves naturally if you find sources of Dexterity later. Piercing Wail(刺耳尖嚎) is invaluable against enemies with multi-hit patterns, or combats with multiple enemies. Cloak and Dagger(斗篷與匕首) and Untouchable(觸不可及) are decent on their own and can offer you directional signals to synergy packages.

Utility

Prepared(早有準備) is the best common Sly enabler. It draws and discards for free, making it easy to activate Sly cards without spending Energy. It is critical to upgrade this card, and it works better the more draw your deck has, so keep that in mind when evaluating it as an early pickup.

Strong Uncommons to Pick With the Silent

Damage Sources

Flechettes(飛鏢) and Pinpoint(精密瞄準) both capitalize on Silent having a lot of Skills in her deck. Pinpoint(精密瞄準) lets you deal damage and gain Block in the same turn without spending Energy on the Attack. Flechettes(飛鏢) combines naturally with the extra cards Ring of the Snake(蛇之戒指) puts in your hand.

Precise Cut(精確切割) and Backstab(背刺) also contribute here as 0-Energy Attacks. Strangle(絞殺) and Finisher(終結技) both scale with how many cards you can play in a turn, making them better the more efficient and synergistic your deck becomes. Predator(獵殺者) and Dash(衝刺) are worth considering if you are desperate for more damage output early, but they fall off as the run develops.

For a Poison package, consider Bouncing Flask(彈跳藥瓶) and Bubble Bubble(咕嘟冒泡).

Defensive

Silent scales exceptionally well with Dexterity, given how many cheap and premium Block cards she has access to alongside the extra Defend(防禦) in her starting deck. This makes Footwork(步法) almost always worth picking up. Leg Sweep(掃腿) is amazing, as Weak is one of the most impactful keywords in the game, and having a card that blocks alongside applying it is great early in your run.

Utility

Well-Laid Plans(計畫妥當) is one of the best consistency cards in the game. Calculated Gamble(精算一搏), Acrobatics(雜技), Reflex(反射神經), and Tactician(戰術家) are the core Discard cards that give Silent more draw and Energy to work with, making your turns more consistent and giving you more output each cycle with your Sly synergies.

Detailed Build Directions for the Silent

The Silent’s starting Relic, Ring of the Snake(蛇之戒指), draws 2 additional cards on the first turn of every combat. Her two unique starter cards, Neutralize(中和) and Survivor(生存者), are defensive tools. The character excels at mitigating damage in the early fights, but her starting deck has very little damage output, so finding meaningful damage is your early priority in Act 1.

Silent has three primary synergistic card packages: Shiv, Poison, and Sly. Each solves different problems. Shiv cards give you a source of frontloaded damage that scales with investment. Poison gives you a damage solution that grows over longer fights without requiring Energy spend each turn. Sly cards give you consistency and efficiency in draw and resource use.

These card packages naturally overlap, and the best runs often combine elements of multiple ones rather than exclusively leaning into one of them. Recognizing the use cases of your early card reward offers and the specific problems your deck still needs to solve will guide you toward the right combination more reliably than committing to a single direction from the start.

Shiv Builds

Shivs are 0-Energy Attack cards. At base, they only deal 4 damage each, but Accuracy(精準) significantly improves them, especially when upgraded. This makes Shivs a reliable frontload damage solution. If you find yourself without a clear primary damage source early and are offered an Accuracy(精準), strongly consider committing to Shivs. Leading Strike(先導打擊) is one of the best early cards for this package, generating a Shiv alongside its base damage, making it a useful pick regardless of whether you are already committed or just keeping your options open.

The other Shiv generators are more situational. Cloak and Dagger(斗篷與匕首) is fine without being exciting. Blade Dance(刀刃之舞) Exhausts on play, which can lead to situations where you hold it on the first cycle to get more value out of your payoff cards before using it. Hidden Daggers(暗藏匕首) generates 2 Shivs for 0 Energy, which is strong but requires discarding 2 cards. This is usually not a problem as the last card you play in a turn, but it’s something to be mindful of when Well-Laid Plans(計畫妥當) or Finisher(終結技) is involved.

As you develop the package further, the recently reworked Blade of Ink(墨刃) is a great card, generating Shivs that apply Weak, extending the value of each hit beyond raw damage. Fan of Knives(刀扇) generates 4 Shivs and makes all Shivs hit all enemies: this is a meaningful upgrade in multi-enemy fights. Knife Trap(刀刃陷阱) is a strong finisher. Infinite Blades(無盡刀刃) gives you a Shiv each turn and is acceptable if you need more generation, but it is a slower contribution compared to the other options here. Shadow Step(暗影步) also offers strong synergies here, as well as “whenever you play a card” cards like Afterimage(殘影), Serpent Form(群蛇形態), or Strangle(絞殺).

You can read more about the build here: The Silent’s Shiv Mechanic: How to Make a Returning Favorite Work

Sly Builds

Sly and Discard cards are the Silent's consistency package, primarily helping her draw more cards and get more output from each turn. Even without leaning into Sly payoffs, cards like an upgraded Prepared(早有準備), Acrobatics(雜技), Calculated Gamble(精算一搏), and Tools of the Trade(行家工具) help you cycle through your deck faster and see your strongest cards more consistently. Adding Sly on top of that (playing those same cards for free when discarded) turns your consistency tools into your most powerful cards.

The primary Sly payoffs are Reflex(反射神經), Tactician(戰術家), and Untouchable(觸不可及). Flick-Flack(翻身撐擊), Ricochet(彈射), and Haze(迷霧) can also contribute small amounts of damage as Sly cards without requiring archetype-level commitment.

Things get really powerful once you start applying Sly to cards that were not designed for it. Hand Trick(掌上戲法) is a flexible tool you can use situationally to get a free play at the right moment. Then, with Master Planner(謀劃專家) in play (provided you can do so, as it is a very slow card). your Block cards, draw cards, and Poison applicators all become free when discarded, which is where Silent starts doing things that feel genuinely broken.

The high volume of draw that this package generates also makes cards like Pinpoint(精密瞄準) and Grand Finale(華麗收場) strong picks.

Poison Builds

Poison is the most iconic build for the class, and it remains fully intact with even more flexibility. It deals its primary damage through Bouncing Flask(彈跳藥瓶), Noxious Fumes(毒霧), and Bubble Bubble(咕嘟冒泡), with Accelerant(催化劑) really speeding things up. Unlike Shivs, which give you immediate frontloaded damage, Poison works best in longer fights where it has time to accumulate.

Poison does not have a strong early directional signal card in the way Accuracy(精準) points you toward Shivs, but keep an eye out for Snecko Skull(異蛇頭骨). Having one of the common Poison cards is not enough on its own to lean toward committing to it as a primary damage source. The cards that define the package are Bouncing Flask(彈跳藥瓶) and Bubble Bubble(咕嘟冒泡). Combined with the draw packages covered in the Sly section, Noxious Fumes(毒霧) and Corrosive Wave(腐蝕波) can mean a significant burst of Poison applied in a single turn rather than building it slowly over multiple cycles.

The main weakness of relying on Poison as your primary damage source is enemies that scale their damage quickly. Fights like Devoted Sculptor and the first stage of Test Subject will outpace your ability to block before Poison finishes them off, meaning you need a higher damage output than what Poison alone can provide in those situations. You should chiefly treat it as supplementary damage rather than your sole answer in combat, except if you have the aforementioned Accelerant(催化劑).

儲君

Regent

StarsSovereign BladeHand/Draw Control

The Regent is one of two brand-new characters in Slay the Spire 2. He’s an arrogant alien heir carried around on a throne by his minions, and his kit is built around generating and spending his exclusive energy resource and using a special sword to deal lots of damage.

Tier Lists for Regent

Exclusive Regent Mechanics

  • Stars - A secondary resource that sits alongside your Energy. Generated by various cards, Stars don’t reset at the end of your turn and have no cap.
  • Forge (and Sovereign Blade) - The Regent’s personal weapon, created the first time you play a card with the Forge keyword each combat. It grows stronger with each Forge.

Regent Builds

Stars Builds

Stars are the Regent’s most fundamental resource, making them one of the most obvious builds. Once generated through cards like Venerate(崇敬), Glow(輝光), and Shining Strike(明耀打擊), you can spend them on Devastate(摧毀) for offense or Reflect(反射) for defense.

Sovereign Blade Builds

Sovereign Blade stacks Forge triggers to build the weapon up into a devastating finisher. Seeking Edge(尋敵鋒刃) and Conqueror(征服者) are the dream combination dealing massive damage. While the blade does have Retain, just keep in mind that you’ll need to fetch it with Summon Forth(召喚現身) after use or wait to draw it again. 

Hand/Draw Control Builds

Hand and draw control rewards you for cycling the same cards repeatedly. Kingly Punch(王者之拳) gets stronger and Kingly Kick(王者之踢) gets cheaper each time you draw them. You can use cards like Cosmic Indifference(宇宙冷漠) and Glimmer(微光) to directly control which cards you want to have in your hand on the next turn.

Exclusive Regent Relics

The Regent’s exclusive Relics mostly focus on Star generation and spending, but the selection also contains some Colorless synergy and automatic Forging at the start of combat.

You can find the full list here.

Exclusive Regent Potions

There are three exclusive Regent potions: free Stars, free Forging, and Colorless card generation.

You can find the full list here.

The Regent's Early Game

The early game of this character is typically centered around finding the powerful cards that use Stars as a resource and securing the Star generation needed to play them more than once in combat.

Astral Pulse(星界脈衝) is a very powerful Common Attack. The Regent's starter deck has 3 base Stars from his Relic but only generates 2 more per cycle through your deck via Venerate(崇敬). This means that you may not be able to play Astral Pulse(星界脈衝) again on your second cycle, unless you draw Venerate(崇敬) first. This is an important constraint, and it means you have to be mindful of how many Star cards you add to your deck.

Strong Commons to pick with the Regent

Attacks:

Astral Pulse(星界脈衝) and Solar Strike(太陽打擊) are the key additions. However, you can not be too picky in your first few floors.

A key exception to your Common choices here is Guiding Star(引路星). It is a good card on the surface: dealing 12 Damage and drawing two cards for just 1 Energy is amazing. However, it does cost 2 Stars to play. There are a lot of more premium Star cards to take, so it can be a strain on your Star generation over time to support this and another premium card alongside it in the early game.

Skills:

From your Common Skills in the early game, you're looking for either Star generation to spend on your strong cards or Block. It's important to still take a Block card even when you're focused on Stars, or else you'll continually take damage in each fight.

Strong Uncommons to Pick With the Regent

Uncommon Attacks:

Gamma Blast(伽瑪轟射): A good source of Vulnerable and Weak on a 0-Energy Attack.

Radiate(輻射): Once you get some Star generation going, this can be a strong source of damage.

Shining Strike(明耀打擊): It can become detrimental later as you lose 1 draw, but it's great when you need the Stars.

Kingly Kick(王者之踢): Not your first choice as an Attack at 4 Energy, but it can be enabled by draw and deck manipulation since it reduces in cost by 1 Energy each time it's drawn. Photon Cut(光子切割) and Cosmic Indifference(宇宙冷漠) can put Kingly Kick(王者之踢) on top of your draw pile to speed up the cost reduction.

Uncommon Skills:

Particle Wall(粒子牆): Just okay until you get Star generation going, but it's a premium Block source later, so it's worth taking.

CHARGE!!: It both makes your deck more consistent later and provides a powerful effect early.

Manifest Authority(彰顯權威): Good jack-of-all-trades tool.

Reflect(反射): It does cost 3 Stars, but blocking for 17 on 1 Energy is very premium.

Bulwark(堡壘): Provides solid Block and helps with damage scaling. One of the best Forge cards.

Uncommon Powers:

Child of the Stars(群星之子): One of the main things you focus on with this character is Stars, so this is a great Block source.

Furnace(熔爐): This can be a good way to deal with the Act 1 boss if you need scaling. Consistently increasing your Sovereign Blade damage could be exactly what you need to clear a boss even without other Forge cards.

Orbit(環繞軌道): If you can upgrade this card, it's one of the few ways the Regent can generate Energy, so it's worth taking.

How to Use the Stars Mechanic

Stars are the resource that separates the Regent from every other character in Slay the Spire 2. Unlike Energy, which resets every turn, Stars must be actively generated and managed across your deck. You start each combat with 3 Stars from Divine Right(神授君權), but adding too many Star-costing cards without the generation to support them will leave you with powerful cards you cannot reliably play.

Best Star Cards to pick:

Comet(彗星) — Functionally, a bigger Gamma Blast(伽瑪轟射), which is already a good choice.

Dying Star(垂死之星) — Powerful card. It does have Ethereal, however, which is somewhat awkward with Star cards: it can be correct to hold off on playing Star cards to make sure you have enough available for more important turns.

Decisions, Decisions(抉擇，抉擇) — This can be very powerful if you have the Stars to play it, and can even leave you ahead on Stars if you use it on something like Hidden Cache(隱密寶庫).

Key Star Generation tools to consider:

For the most part, these cards don't do much beyond generating Stars and costing Energy to play. This is one of the main reasons why it's so important to primarily take premium Star cards and generation, and to not take too much of either. Both Energy and Stars are limited resources you have to manage well to succeed with the Regent.

Niche cards with narrow use cases:

Crescent Spear(新月長矛) — This card gets stronger the more Star cards you have, but if you have multiple Star cards, your deck should already be strong, so its uses are limited.

Devastate(摧毀) and Seven Stars(七星) — While these cards do deal a lot of damage for their Energy cost, you will need a lot of Star generation to support it, which is a challenge.

How to Use the Forge Mechanic

Forge is the Regent's other scaling mechanic and works alongside your Star cards rather than competing with them. Unlike Stars, which require constant generation and management, Forge is more of a passive scaling tool that rewards you for taking certain cards without demanding you build around it exclusively.

Sovereign Blade starts at 10 damage, but with a few Forge cards picked up naturally over a run, it can become a reliable finisher that opponents have to respect. The key cards to know are the ones that provide Forge efficiently enough to be worth the deck slot on their own merit, rather than requiring full commitment to see results.

Key Forge Cards to Consider:

Bulwark(堡壘) — The glue that lets you really consider playing around Sovereign Blade. Since it's a fine Block card on its own merits, the Forge value is a great bonus. Even without committing more than 3 slots toward Sovereign Blade, with 2 Bulwark(堡壘) and a Furnace(熔爐), Sovereign Blade can do very respectable damage.

Furnace(熔爐) — As mentioned above, this is something I'd primarily take to have enough damage for an early boss. That's still the primary situation I'd take it in unless I have other clear signals to lean into Forge.

Seeking Edge(尋敵鋒刃) — 1 Energy, Forge 7. Sovereign Blade now deals damage to ALL enemies. This is something you may transform into and see early, which makes it a bit more tempting to lean into Sovereign Blade. However, the Regent has Astral Pulse(星界脈衝), which does 14 AOE for 3 Stars as a single Common card — versus 17 damage for 3 Energy on a Rare card, so Seeking Edge(尋敵鋒刃) by itself isn't enough compared to other options the Regent has.

The Smith(鍛匠) — 1 Energy, 4 Stars, Forge 30. This is a big amount of Forge and worth considering building around. It's comparable to Devastate(摧毀), which does its damage right away and doesn't require 2 more Energy spent. However, Sovereign Blade does have Retain, so it can better line up with damage-amplifying effects like Vulnerable.

Conqueror(征服者) — Powerful effect for Sovereign Blade builds, allowing you to double its damage. However, some enemies counter this playstyle: Skulking Colony, the Cockroaches, or Slippery enemies all limit how much damage you can do in a single Attack.

Summon Forth(召喚現身) — Forge 8. Puts Sovereign Blade into your hand from anywhere. A great card to get Sovereign Blade back for a second Attack, but as a 2-cost Attack with Retain, it'll typically want to be used as a finishing blow.

Forge is worth paying attention to when the cards justify themselves independently. Bulwark(堡壘) is the clearest example of a card you take for its Block value and get Forge as a bonus. Beyond that, the degree to which you invest in Sovereign Blade depends on what the run offers you. A few naturally acquired Forge cards can turn Sovereign Blade into a meaningful finisher without demanding full commitment, but chasing Forge at the expense of Star cards and generation is rarely the right call.

縛亡者

Necrobinder

OstyDoomSouls

The Necrobinder is one of two brand-new characters in Slay the Spire 2. She’s a lich who fights alongside Osty, a giant reanimated hand, and her kit is built around three interlocking systems: building up and using Osty as both a shield and a weapon, stacking Doom on enemies as a quasi-instant kill, and churning through Souls for card draw. Like the Regent, everything about how she plays is entirely new to the series.

Tier Lists for Necrobinder

Exclusive Necrobinder Mechanics

The Necrobinder does have some exclusive mechanics to learn:

  • Summon - A keyword that, strengthens the Necrobinder’s minion, Osty. Osty can attack enemies independently, allowing him to keep dealing damage even if you’re debuffed. He also acts as a shield, intercepting Attacks that would hit you, even to the point of its own death.
  • Doom - A stacking effect similar to Poison, but instead of dealing damage each turn, Doom instantly kills an enemy if its total value ever exceeds their maximum HP at the end of their turn.
  • Souls - Generated cards that cost nothing, draw two cards, and are then Exhausted.

Necrobinder Builds

Osty Builds

Osty builds focus on empowering your minion as much as possible. This can mean buffing him with cards like Reanimate(復生) and then dealing heavy damage with Unleash(解縛出擊), or maximizing the number of hits he delivers and pairing that with effects like Rattle(猛晃) for rapid multi-hit pressure.

Doom Builds

Aim to stack Doom quickly with cards that accelerate the Doom applied to enemies, or big Block cards that help address the downside of Doom killing after the enemy's turn.

Soul Builds

Use generated Soul cards to rapidly cycle through your deck, with bonus payoffs for playing them that can fuel either damage or Osty's HP depending on your other cards. Combine overall strong Necrobinder cards with Soul-specific ones like Haunt(作祟) and Soul Storm(靈魂風暴).

Ethereal Builds

Before Necrobinder, Ethereal was mainly a downside that exhausted your powerful cards if you could not play them on a turn. Now, Ethereal builds turn a traditional weakness into a strength, using cards that get stronger or cheaper the more Ethereal cards you play.

Understanding the Necrobinder

Early Game

The cards highlighted below are good regardless of which direction your run takes, but understanding how they connect to the archetypes outlined above will help you make better decisions when card rewards start pointing you in a specific direction.

Strong Commons to Pick With the Necrobinder

Attacks: Poke(戳刺), Blight Strike(荒疫打擊), Defile(褻瀆), Reave(剝奪), Fear(驚懼): Fear(驚懼) is especially worth mentioning. Vulnerable is a very strong effect in Slay the Spire 2, increasing your attack damage by 50%. A lot of the difficult fights in the early game (Elites) are damage checks, so dealing more damage is key.

Skills: Defy(違逆), Grave Warden(墓穴守衛), Negative Pulse(負能脈衝), Scourge(鞭笞): Defy(違逆), like Fear(驚懼), also debuffs enemies, but this time with Weak. Weak is one of the most important keywords throughout a run; enemies dealing 25% less damage is always valuable. You can find other ways to deal damage, but Weak is one of the most efficient and attainable methods of reducing enemy damage.

Strong Uncommons to Pick With the Necrobinder

Attacks:

  • Fetch(取回): 0-cost Attack that replaces itself; a bridge towards Osty.
  • Bone Shards(碎骨): Good AOE damage and Block.
  • Debilitate(摧殘): Doubling Weak and Vulnerable is very strong and can carry runs on its own.
  • High Five(擊掌): AOE and Vulnerable; another generically okay Osty card.

Skills:

  • Borrowed Time(預支時間): Good source of Energy when you will be limited to 3 for most of the run.
  • Dirge(輓歌): Great source of Souls and Summon.
  • Capture Spirit(捕捉靈魂): Strong Souls source that adds consistency.
  • Enfeebling Touch(衰弱之觸): Reducing enemies' attack works similarly to Block most of the time, but is excellent against multi-hit enemies.
  • Deathbringer(死亡使者): Large AOE number and Weak, which we already mentioned as important.
  • Putrefy(腐敗): Source of Vulnerable and Weak. It does Exhaust, so you will need other sources.

Powers:

  • Countdown(倒數計時): This card is just okay, but if you are low on damage it can help you get through a boss fight with its consistent output. It is also a reliable applicator of Doom for cards that synergize with it.
  • Friendship(友誼): Amazing. Energy is scarce, and gaining 1 more Energy a turn allows you to have more powerful turns.
  • Lethality(殺傷性): Great card for the early game when most of your damage still comes from Attacks. It also synergizes with the Ethereal payoff cards.

Detailed Build Directions for the Necrobinder

When starting out, the best approach is to commit to whichever direction your early card rewards push you toward. This leads to more consistent and rewarding runs as you learn the character. As you gain experience, you will start to recognize when to pivot between directions, but for now, the most important thing is understanding what each direction is trying to accomplish and which cards will get you there.

Osty

Osty builds revolve around Osty's Attacks, which synergize with each other, and using the Summon mechanic to have a big Osty that will tank for you and deal increasing damage with the Unleash(解縛出擊) starter card.

Payoffs:

  • Squeeze(緊握): An expensive Attack at 3 Energy, but it can deal a lot of damage, dealing 5 (6) extra damage for ALL of your other Osty Attacks.
  • Rattle(猛晃) and Sic 'Em: Both these Attacks benefit from the number of times Osty has dealt damage this turn, incentivizing you to play a bunch of Osty Attacks.
  • Unleash(解縛出擊): Worth mentioning once again, but this is your main payoff for the Summon mechanic outside of reducing the damage you take, and luckily, you start with it.

Support: The Osty build has fewer early signals than other archetypes, making it slightly harder to identify and commit to. The best bridge card is Fetch(取回), a 0-cost Attack that replaces itself by drawing a card. Its 0-Energy cost means it contributes to your Osty Attack count without eating into your resources. Poke(戳刺) and Flatten(砸扁) are honorable mentions as additional 0-cost Osty Attacks that feed into your payoff cards.

The other key support card worth committing a deck slot to is Calcify(鈣化). This compounds quickly with 0-cost Attacks like Fetch(取回) and Poke(戳刺), and becomes especially strong alongside Rattle(猛晃)'s multiple hits.

Perhaps the clearest signal to commit to this direction is seeing two Fetches early. The combination of free cycling and Osty Attack synergy gives you the foundation to make the payoff cards worth building toward.

Doom

Doom builds revolve around debuffing the enemy with Doom, an above-curve damage source with the downside of allowing enemies to complete their turn before killing them. This means leaning towards defensive cards is key.

Payoffs:

  • No Escape(無處可逃): This card applies more Doom for every 10 Doom on the enemy, giving you ways to accelerate Doom against high-health enemies.
  • Death's Door: An amazing Block card with the potential to block 18-21 damage for just 1 Energy. When blocking this much, the downside of enemies still having a turn can be mostly negated.

Support: With just two main payoff cards, Doom can sometimes feel like something splashed into other builds rather than a primary game plan. However, there are situations where it is still the main thing your deck is doing, and it has some of the strongest standalone commons of any archetype, making it easy to accumulate Doom cards naturally over a run.

Sleight of Flesh(血肉戲法), while not a Doom card itself, combined with the frequency that Doom cards apply debuffs, can become a significant damage source that complements the archetype naturally.

End of Days(末日) is another strong option that partially addresses Doom's main downside. By applying large amounts of AOE Doom and immediately executing any enemy that already meets the threshold, it can single-handedly end fights and avoid the wait for Doom to trigger.

In summary, Doom has enough strong standalone cards in Blight Strike(荒疫打擊), Negative Pulse(負能脈衝), and Scourge(鞭笞) that you will often find yourself with a foundation without actively seeking it. If you then find No Escape(無處可逃), Death's Door, or Sleight of Flesh(血肉戲法), you have a signal that Doom is worth building around.

故障機器人

Defect

OrbsClawUproar

The Defect is one of three returning characters in Slay the Spire 2. It’s a robot that fights by slotting elemental Orbs into a queue that both passively and actively generates damage, Block, and more. Builds range from methodical Orb management to spamming low-cost Attacks.

What's Changed in Slay the Spire 2?

There are a few big changes:

  • The all-new Glass orb does 4 damage to all enemies, decreasing by 1 each turn until it reaches 0, at which point it stops functioning. Evoking it will deal 8 damage to every foe.
  • Status card synergy has been improved via cards like Smokestack(煙囪), Iteration(迭代), and Trash to Treasure(化腐朽為神奇).
  • Unique orb count is more explicitly rewarded through Coolant(冷卻液), Compile Driver(編譯衝擊), and Synchronize(同步).

Defect Builds

Orb and Focus Builds

The Defect’s Orb and Focus build is the most reliable playstyle. Stack(堆疊) Focus through Defragment(碎片整理) to amplify every Orb’s passive and Evoke values; use Frost as needed for defense and Lightning for offense. Prioritize Focus upgrades early and manage your Orb slot count intentionally: having more slots isn’t always better.

  • Flare explains the Orb mechanic and its general key cards
  • Baalorlord's Defect Orb Spam build guide

Claw(爪擊) Builds

Claw(爪擊) builds are all about spamming the common 0-cost Claw(爪擊) card and drawing it as much as possible while cycling through your deck. With damage increasing each time it’s played, you almost completely ignore the Orb mechanic. With All for One(全為一), you can reliably return it to your hand, but the build is a bit inconsistent without it.

Uproar(騷動) Builds

Uproar(騷動) builds want to thin their deck as much as possible to the point that Uproar(騷動) is the only Attack card in it. That way your Uproar(騷動) chains into another Uproar(騷動) and into yet another Uproar(騷動) and so on. It’s really consistent if you can get it up and running, but it does take purposeful setup.

Exclusive Defect Mechanics

The Defect does have some exclusive mechanics to learn:

  • Orbs - The Defect’s signature mechanic. Orbs sit in slots and trigger a passive effect at the end of every turn. When a new Orb is Channeled into a full queue, the rightmost Orb Evokes, triggering a stronger effect one time before it is removed. There are five types: Lightning deals damage, Frost generates Block, Dark builds up over time before being Evoked for large amounts of damage, Plasma generates extra Energy, and Glass deals AOE.
  • Focus - A stat exclusive to the Defect that increases the numerical value of every Orb’s passive and Evoke effects.
  • Channel - The keyword for adding an Orb into your queue.
  • Evoke - The keyword for triggering an Orb’s active effect and removing it from the queue.

The Defect's Early Game

The Defect's starting Relic, Cracked Core(破損核心), Channels one Lightning Orb at the start of each combat. Combined with the two unique starter cards Zap(電擊), which Channels a Lightning Orb for 1 Energy, and Dualcast(雙重施放), which Evokes your next Orb twice for 1 Energy, the Defect has reliable passive damage from the very first turn of a run. This gives you flexibility in the early game to Block and deal damage simultaneously, or burst enemies down with Dualcast(雙重施放) when needed.

For the first few picks, you are generally just taking the strongest individual card on offer. Occasionally the strongest card points toward a less common strategy like Claw(爪擊) and Feral(野性) combos, and that is where runs can branch off into something unexpected.

The next section will cover the Commons and Uncommons worth taking in the early game to set you up for success against the Act 1 Elites.

Strong Commons to Pick With the Defect

Attacks:

  • Cold Snap(寒流): One of the best Commons you can see. Generating a Frost Orb early reduces the chip damage you take from early fights significantly.
  • Ball Lightning(球狀閃電): Generates a Lightning Orb. Filling your starting three Orb slots is when Defect is at its strongest early, and this establishes the core game plan from the very first floor: passive Lightning damage while Frost Orbs handle your Block.
  • Barrage(彈幕): If you can generate Orbs reliably, Barrage(彈幕) deals 15 damage for 1 Energy, which is premium damage for the early game.
  • Sweeping Beam(掃射光束): 6 damage to all enemies plus a draw is fine for the early game, especially against multi-enemy fights.

The following three require an upgrade to properly function, so only pick if you can see yourself doing so at a nearby Rest Site:

  • Focused Strike(集中打擊): 9 damage and 1 temporary Focus provide enough value to pick unupgraded in the short term, but long-term, a basic copy starts to feel like a liability.
  • Beam Cell(光束電池): Often overlooked. Even in its basic form, it deals 6 to 9 damage for 0 Energy, which beats skipping.
  • Go for the Eyes(瞄準眼睛): Only pick this card if you can upgrade it. One turn of Weak is not enough to warrant a deck slot.

Skills:

  • Boost Away(推進脫離): A 0-cost Block card is always useful early. The Dazed it generates is a minor downside worth accepting for the efficiency, and gives you a reason to pick up Status synergy cards later, which is a new avenue of play for Defect in StS2.
  • Hotfix(緊急修正): 2 temporary Focus increases the effectiveness of your Orbs. Exhausts when unupgraded, so plan to upgrade it.
  • Turbo: Gain 2 Energy. Not always a priority pick early, but becomes more valuable as your deck develops and your turns get more demanding.
  • Charge Battery(充電): A premium Block common that generates Energy next turn. Lets you Block and still have output, which is exactly what you want early.
  • Coolheaded(冷靜頭腦): Generating Frost is good. Slightly worse than Cold Snap(寒流) early, but becomes better as the impact of 6 damage lessens throughout a run.

Key Uncommons to Pick With the Defect

Attacks:

  • FTL(超光速): 0-Energy attack that replaces itself. No downside to adding this in.
  • Tesla Coil(特斯拉線圈): Good source of damage early if you have Lightning generation.
  • Null(歸零): Premium. Deals damage and effectively Blocks by applying Weak.
  • Sunder(裂解): A big attack that helps in multi-enemy fights or as a finisher.

Skills:

  • Chill(冰寒): Premium. Generates Frost Orbs for 0 Energy, and generates one for each enemy in multi-enemy fights.
  • Compact(壓縮): Blocks and transforms Status cards into fuel that draws a card and gains Energy. Look for this if fighting Soul Fysh or Vantom as your Act 1 boss, or if you pick up multiple Status-generating cards.
  • Fight Through(強撐): Dense Block, 13 for 1 Energy. Can lead you into a Status-based direction.
  • Glasswork(玻璃工藝): Blocks and generates a Glass Orb. Useful for multi-enemy coverage.
  • Glacier(冰川): Generates two Frost Orbs, really helpful for filling slots with recurring passive Block.
  • Shadow Shield(暗影護盾): Blocks 11 and generates a Dark Orb. Block and damage in one card.

Powers:

Uncommon Powers are situational. Keep in mind your current run state when deciding whether to add one to your deck.

  • Capacitor(擴容): More Orb slots are powerful, but if you do not have the Orbs to fill them, the payoff is delayed and this will be a dead or low-impact card until then.
  • Hailstorm(冰雹風暴): Great passive damage if you have consistent Frost access.
  • Iteration(迭代): Depends on the boss or on having multiple Status cards.
  • Loop(循環): Underwhelming in its basic form. Best with permanent Focus or Dark Orbs.
  • Smokestack(煙囪): Decent damage with multiple Status cards.
  • Bulk Up(強化體魄): Good if you can give up an Orb slot and afford 2 Energy to put it in play.
  • Feral(野性): This card has a chicken-and-egg relationship with Claw(爪擊). Build around this primarily after a transform or if you have one of the debuff attacks you want to replay.
